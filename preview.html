Hengjie Liu

Postdoctoral Scholar · UCSF Radiation Oncology

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in Radiation Oncology at the University of California, San Francisco. I received my Ph.D. in Physics & Biology in Medicine from UCLA and my B.S. in Engineering Physics from Tsinghua University. My research broadly focuses on computational methods for medical imaging.

My research training has spanned two fairly different areas. I spent about four years working on MRI physics, acquisition, and reconstruction, including 4D MRI, 4D flow MRI, pulse-sequence development, and low-field MRI systems. I subsequently spent about four years working on deformable image registration, with an emphasis on learning- and optimization-based methods, registration-specific design, multimodal registration, and anatomically plausible deformation.

Moving between these areas required me to learn new tools and ways of thinking. I am particularly interested in problems at the intersection of medical imaging, machine learning, numerical optimization, and imaging physics, and in understanding when a computational method works—not only whether it works.

Medical Image Analysis, 2025

MUSA (MUsculo-Skeleton-Aware)

Anatomically Guided Head-and-Neck Deformable Registration

MUsculo-Skeleton-Aware (MUSA) is a two-stage registration framework designed for large and anatomically complex head-and-neck deformation.

  • Decomposes deformation into a bulk posture change and residual fine deformation.
  • Uses anatomical information to encourage different deformation behavior in bony structures and soft tissue.
  • Emphasizes deformation plausibility in addition to registration accuracy.

MIDL — Short Papers, 2025

Registration-Specific Design Before Architectural Complexity

Unsupervised Deformable Image Registration Revisited

This project studies which registration-specific design choices are most important for learning-based deformable image registration.

  • Evaluates multi-resolution estimation, local correlation, and inverse consistency.
  • Tests registration design choices in controlled experiments.
  • Includes validation across LUMIR and OASIS.

MICCAI 2026 Off-Grid Workshop — Oral

The Right Prior for the Right Deformation

Rethinking Continuous Deformable Image Registration

This project studies how continuous deformable image registration methods induce different deformation priors through their parameterization and optimization.

  • Compares dense implicit neural representations and B-Spline control-point parameterizations across OASIS brain MRI and DIR-LAB 4DCT.
  • Evaluates how locality, smoothness, scale, and multiresolution structure affect registration across different deformation regimes.
  • Shows that matching the induced deformation prior to the target motion pattern improves performance, with MR-D-BSCP performing best overall.
