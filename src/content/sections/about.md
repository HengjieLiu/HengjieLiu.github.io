I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in Radiation Oncology at the University of California, San Francisco. I received my Ph.D. in Physics & Biology in Medicine from UCLA and my B.S. in Engineering Physics from Tsinghua University. My research broadly focuses on computational methods for medical imaging and clinical applications.

My research training has spanned two fairly different areas. I spent about four years working on MRI physics, acquisition, and reconstruction, including pulse sequence programming, 4D cardiac MRI, 4D flow MRI, and low-field portable and programmable MRI systems. I subsequently spent about five years till now working on deformable image registration, spanning both learning- and optimization-based methods, with an emphasis registration-specific designs, multimodal registration, and anatomically plausible and clinically trustworthy deformation.

Moving between these areas required me to learn new tools and new ways of thinking quickly. I am particularly interested in problems at the intersection of medical imaging, machine learning, numerical optimization, and imaging physics, and in understanding when a computational method works—not only whether it works.
